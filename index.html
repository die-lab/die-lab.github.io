Diego Carli

Research fellow
Facoltà di Scienze Agrarie, Alimentari e Ambientali – DiSTAS
Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, Piacenza, Italy

I study how the nuclear and mitochondrial genomes talk to each other. My main interest is small RNAs of mitochondrial origin (smithRNAs): how to find them in sequencing data, how widespread they are across animals, and what they may regulate. I develop bioinformatic pipelines for small RNA-seq and apply them to questions in evolutionary genomics, from the role of mitochondria in sex determination in bivalves to the possible bacterial origin of these regulatory elements in Wolbachia.

Projects

SmithHunter2

PythonGPL-3.0early development

Finds small RNA loci on every genome present in a sample (nuclear, mitochondrial, symbionts, contaminants). Each read is assigned to the genome it fits best, so mitochondrial reads are not lost to nuclear copies (NUMTs) and vice versa. It is a rewrite of SmithHunter, no longer limited to mitochondria.

Bivalve mitogenome reannotation

Pythonmethods

A step-by-step, reusable workflow to retrieve complete mitochondrial genomes, extract the 13 protein-coding genes and build a new backbone for mitochondrial protein annotation in the class Bivalvia.
